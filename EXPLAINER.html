The Big Picture

What is this thing, really?

oa2 is a computer program that watches 22 well-known stocks and exchange-traded funds (ETFs) every day, decides whether it makes sense to place an options trade on any of them, figures out exactly how large that trade should be, places the order automatically, and then watches the trade around the clock until it's time to close it.

It does all of this by running the market data through 9 specialised processing steps — like an assembly line — before a single trade is ever placed. Each step either adds more information or acts as a safety check. A trade only happens when every step gives the green light.

Think of it like a very careful restaurant kitchen. Before a dish leaves the kitchen it passes through the prep cook, the line cook, the sous chef, and the head chef — each checking something different. Only a perfect dish gets served. oa2 works the same way: a trade only reaches the customer (the market) after passing through every station.

What Is an Options Trade?

A quick plain-English primer

A stock option is a contract that gives you the right (but not the obligation) to buy or sell a stock at a specific price, before a specific date. You pay a small premium upfront — like buying a ticket — and your maximum loss is just that ticket price.

Imagine you think a house currently worth $300,000 will be worth $350,000 in three months. Instead of buying the whole house (expensive!), you pay $2,000 to lock in the right to buy it at $300,000 within three months. If the price goes to $350,000, you make a big profit. If it doesn't, you just lose your $2,000 ticket price — not the whole house.

Why options instead of just buying stocks?

Options let you profit from how a stock moves, not just whether it goes up. You can make money when a stock stays flat (by collecting premium), when it falls, when it gets more volatile, or when it gets less volatile. oa2 exploits all of these scenarios — choosing the right type of options trade for the current market environment.

Key terms you'll see

TermPlain English
DTE (Days to Expiration)How many days before the option contract expires. Like the "best before" date on food — closer to zero means the option decays faster.
PremiumThe price you pay for the option contract — like a ticket price.
IV Rank (Implied Volatility Rank)How expensive options are right now vs their history. High IV Rank = expensive options = good time to sell. Low IV Rank = cheap options = good time to buy.

SpreadBuying one option and selling another to limit both risk and cost — like buying a discounted round-trip flight instead of open-ended travel.

The 9-Layer Assembly Line

Every trade passes through all 9 stations

When oa2 evaluates any stock, the market data travels through 9 layers from top to bottom. Earlier layers provide context; later layers make decisions. Think of them as different specialists in an operating room, each doing their part before the surgeon makes the final cut.

L0
Market Data — Gathering the raw ingredients
Collects price, volume, options chain, news, social media sentiment, and flow data from multiple sources.
Like a shopper going to multiple grocery stores to get the freshest ingredients for dinner.
L1
Regime Detector — Reading the weather
Classifies the current market "weather" into one of 8 buckets based on volatility and trend. Is it calm? Stormy? Trending or choppy?
Like a farmer checking the weather before deciding which crops to plant today.
L2
Context Agents — Reading the room
Four read-only specialists check: big-bank positioning (GEX), macro conditions, upcoming events (earnings, Fed meetings), and whether it's actually easy to trade right now.
Like checking: what are the big players doing? Is anything major happening this week? Is the highway even open?
L3
Timeframe Router — Picking the right clock
Decides whether to look at intraday signals, daily signals, or weekly signals for this particular stock at this moment.
Like deciding whether to check traffic every 5 minutes or just once a day depending on your trip length.
L4
Debater Ensemble — 6 experts give opinions
Six independent expert modules each look at different aspects of the market and vote: Bullish, Bearish, or Neutral — each with a confidence level.
Like asking 6 different doctors to diagnose a patient. Each specialises in something different. None of them can see the others' diagnosis until they've made their own.
L5
Consensus Engine — Counting the votes properly
Combines the 6 opinions into one final direction. But not all votes are equal — experts who tend to agree with each other are down-weighted to avoid double-counting. Past accuracy also matters.
Like a jury that gives more weight to witnesses with track records of being right, and less weight when two witnesses are clearly just repeating each other.
L6
Sizing Engine — How many contracts to buy
Three consecutive safety checks determine exactly how many contracts to trade: Kelly formula (edge-based), Greeks budget (whole-portfolio balance), and CVaR stress test (worst-case scenario check).
Like a casino asking: given your edge, your current table exposure, and what happens if there's an earthquake — how many chips should you bet?
L7
Portfolio Orchestrator — Keeping the book balanced
Tracks all open trades together and ensures the whole portfolio doesn't get too lopsided — not too directional, not too much volatility exposure, not too much time decay.
Like making sure your investment portfolio isn't all in tech stocks even if every individual tech stock looks like a great deal.
L8
Execution & Learning — Place the trade, then watch it
Places the order via moomoo broker, assigns exit rules to every trade at birth, monitors all open positions in real time, and learns from what worked and what didn't.
Like a pilot who not only takes off but also monitors the flight continuously, has autopilot rules for turbulence, and reviews the flight data after landing.

Reading the Market "Weather"

Why the same trade can be great or terrible depending on conditions

Markets cycle through different "weather" conditions just like the real sky. Selling a short-premium options trade when the market is panicking is like selling umbrellas right before the storm hits — your inventory gets wiped out. Selling premium in a calm trending market? Profitable and predictable.

oa2 classifies the current environment into one of 8 buckets by crossing two measurements:

📉 Volatility Compression
Options are cheap. The market is quiet. Like a calm summer day — good for buying protection cheaply, bad for selling it.
😐 Normal Volatility
Standard market conditions. Neither cheap nor expensive. The "partly cloudy" of market weather — standard playbook.
🌩️ Volatility Expansion
Options are expensive — fear is elevated. Premium selling opportunity: collect fat premiums, but manage risk tightly.
🚨 Crisis
VIX above 35 or real volatility outpacing implied. Like a hurricane warning — drastically smaller positions, defensive trades only. Overrides everything else.

Each of these is crossed with the trend state: is the market trending (clear direction), mean-reverting (bouncing between levels), or neutral (undecided)? That gives 8 combinations — 4 volatility states × 2 main trend states (simplified).

Intraday session overlay

On top of the daily regime, oa2 also tracks time of day. The first 30 minutes after the opening bell (9:30–10:00 AM) behave completely differently from midday (12:00–2:00 PM). At the open, big orders are landing, flows are meaningful, and prices jump around. At midday, it's sleepy — perfect for slow theta collection. The system adjusts which experts to trust based on the time of day.

A surfer reads both the swell (daily regime) and the tide schedule (time of day). The same beach can be great at low tide and dangerous at high tide. oa2 does the same thing with market conditions.

The 6 Expert "Debaters"

Each looks at a completely different piece of evidence

Before any vote is taken, 6 independent expert modules each examine the market data through their own specialised lens and return a verdict: Bullish (expect stock to rise), Bearish (expect it to fall), or Neutral (not enough evidence). Each verdict comes with a confidence score from 0.0 (no idea) to 1.0 (very confident).

Crucially, each expert is not allowed to see the others' verdicts before forming their own. This prevents herd thinking — if one expert is confidently wrong, the others won't blindly follow.

📈 Directional
Question: which way is price moving?
Looks at: price vs moving averages, momentum, breakouts.
Like a track coach who watches if the runner is accelerating or slowing down.
💰 Income
Question: is this a good time to sell premium?
Looks at: how expensive options are (IV rank), skew, term structure.
Like an insurance salesperson checking if premiums are high enough to justify the risk.
🌊 Volatility
Question: is vol going up or down?
Looks at: VIX, vol-of-vol (VVIX), term structure shape.
Like a meteorologist who specialises in storm forecasting — not where you'll go, but how rough the ride will be.
🐋 Flow
Question: what are the big money traders doing?
Looks at: call/put sweeps, dark pool prints, OI changes.
Like watching where the whales swim — if big funds are quietly buying calls, that's a signal.
⚠️ Abstains completely if no real-time tape data is available. No fake signals.
📱 Sentiment
Question: what is the crowd feeling?
Looks at: Reddit (r/wallstreetbets), StockTwits, news headlines.
Like a town hall meeting — you can't trade on Twitter rumours alone, but crowd panic or euphoria is a real signal.
🏦 Dealer
Question: what position are market makers holding?
Looks at: Gamma Exposure (GEX), call walls, put walls, max pain. Only active for SPY/QQQ/IWM.
Like knowing what position the casino dealer is holding — market makers hedge mechanically, which creates predictable price magnetism.
Honest abstention rule: any debater who doesn't have high-quality data on a given day must say "I don't know" instead of guessing. The flow debater, for example, returns a confidence of exactly 0.0 if real-time order flow isn't available. The system is designed to be wrong less often by saying nothing when it doesn't know.

Counting the Votes — The Consensus Engine

Not all votes are created equal

When all 6 experts have voted, the Consensus Engine combines them into a single direction and confidence score. But this is not a simple average. Three things make it smarter:

1. Correlated experts get down-weighted

If two experts tend to always agree with each other, their combined vote only counts as roughly one vote — not two. They're probably looking at related data (e.g., price momentum and moving-average crossover both come from the same price series). Treating them as independent would inflate your confidence.

Imagine asking five friends whether to bring an umbrella. If three of them are checking the same weather app, that's really only two independent opinions (the three app-checkers count as one). A smart jury weighs independent sources more heavily.

2. High-conviction experts get more weight

An expert who says "BULLISH with 90% confidence" gets more influence than one who says "BULLISH with 30% confidence." A doctor who says "I'm almost certain this is appendicitis" should outweigh a colleague who says "it might be appendicitis, maybe."

3. Track record matters — the Bandit

Over time, the system tracks which debater was right in which market regime. For example, the flow debater might be very accurate when the market is in a TRENDING + NORMAL state, but unreliable in CRISIS mode. The consensus engine uses a Thompson sampling bandit to automatically give more weight to whoever has been most accurate in the current regime.

Think of a sports team with 6 specialists. You wouldn't use your sprinter in a swimming race. The bandit is the coach who knows which player performs best in which conditions and adjusts the starting lineup accordingly.

How Much to Trade — The Sizing Engine

The hardest problem in trading: how many contracts?

Having a correct direction is only half the battle. Betting too much on a correct trade can still wipe you out if the timing is off. Betting too little wastes the opportunity. The sizing engine runs three consecutive checks — a trade only gets approved if it passes all three.

①
Kelly Formula — "How much does your edge say to bet?"
Uses the consensus probability (e.g., 72%) and the trade's payout ratio (max profit vs max loss) to calculate the mathematically optimal bet size. Uses ¼-Kelly (25% of the formula) to stay conservative. Adjusts down further if the trade is close to expiration.
②
Greeks Budget — "Is the whole portfolio still balanced?"
Checks the proposed trade against the entire book of open positions. Hard limits: net directional exposure cannot exceed 30% of account; daily time-decay cannot exceed 2% of account; no single stock can dominate more than 25% of the portfolio's risk. If adding this trade would breach any cap, the size is reduced or the trade is rejected.
③
CVaR Stress Test — "What's the worst realistic case?"
Runs 5 nightmare scenarios before approving: stock drops 3%, stock drops 5%, VIX spikes +10, VIX spikes +20 (crisis), and every position moves against you at once. If any scenario would lose more than 5% of the account, the size is cut until it fits — or the trade is rejected entirely.
Think of a pilot's pre-flight checklist. Gate ① is "do we have enough fuel for the trip?" Gate ② is "is the weight distribution safe?" Gate ③ is "what happens if we hit severe turbulence or lose an engine?" Every gate must be cleared before wheels-up. No single opinion can override all three checks.

DTE-aware sizing

Options close to expiration are like ice cream left on a warm counter — they deteriorate fast. An option with 1 day left can lose all its value overnight. oa2 automatically reduces the trade size as expiration approaches: full size from 7–45 days out, 75% at 46+ days, 75% at 3–6 days, and only 50% in the last 2 days (where gamma makes options behave erratically).

Knowing When to Sell — The Exit Engine

Every trade has a birth certificate and a scheduled check-up

The moment a trade is approved, the exit engine immediately assigns it exit conditions — like discharge instructions after a hospital procedure. These are checked against every open position continuously during market hours.

Six rules run in priority order. When the first rule fires, action is taken immediately — the lower rules don't even get evaluated:

🛑 Hard Stop Loss
Loss reaches the maximum pre-defined dollar amount → close immediately, no questions asked.
🎯 Profit Target
Gain reaches 50% of maximum possible profit → close and bank it. Don't get greedy and give it back.
⏰ DTE Emergency
Options expire in fewer than 2 days → close immediately to avoid assignment (being forced to buy/sell 100 shares).
📅 Time Stop
Position has been held longer than planned (e.g., 21 days) → evaluate whether to hold or fold.
🔔 Hard EOD Close
3:55 PM ET every day → all intraday positions are force-closed before the market shuts.
🔄 Regime Flip
Market conditions changed (e.g., went from calm to crisis) → re-run the experts; if they now disagree with the trade direction, close it.
Think of these as the rules your babysitter has: (1) If the baby cries for more than X minutes, call the parents. (2) If the baby finishes dinner, give dessert and call it done. (3) If bedtime arrives, put the baby to sleep regardless. Rules are set in advance precisely so that panic doesn't lead to bad real-time decisions.

Roll logic

When a short-premium trade is doing well (collected over 25% of its maximum profit), has 2 weeks or less before expiration, and the market conditions haven't changed — the system evaluates whether to "roll" it forward: close the current trade and open a fresh one at a later expiration date to collect more premium, instead of just closing outright.

Getting Smarter Over Time — The Bandit

The system that learns which expert to trust and when

oa2 uses a Thompson sampling bandit — a type of learning algorithm — to track which debater has been most accurate in each market regime. There are 6 debaters and 8 regime buckets, giving 48 separate "track records" maintained independently.

Each track record is a Beta distribution — a probability estimate that gets updated every time a debater's call is right or wrong. When the bandit is confident that "the income debater is very reliable in HIGH VOLATILITY + TRENDING markets," it gives that debater more weight in the consensus engine for those conditions — automatically, without human intervention.

Imagine a basketball coach tracking each player's shooting percentage for every court zone (left corner, right elbow, paint, etc.). Over time, the coach knows Player A is best from the left corner and Player B is best from three-point range. The coach adjusts the plays accordingly. The bandit is that coach — except it's tracking 48 zone/player combinations simultaneously and never forgets a statistic.

Cold-start problem

A brand-new system has no history — so the bandit has no useful statistics yet. To solve this, oa2 pre-loads 6 months of historical data before going live: it replays every day of the past 6 months through the full system, records what each debater would have said, compares it to what actually happened next day, and uses that to seed the bandit's statistics. This is called "warm-starting" and means the system begins its first real day of trading with meaningful beliefs rather than starting from scratch.

EWMA correlation tracker

The consensus engine also tracks how correlated the debaters are to each other using EWMA (Exponentially Weighted Moving Average). Recent observations matter more than old ones (decay factor 0.94 — each new observation is ~6% of the update). This means if two debaters started diverging from each other recently, the system recognises this quickly and adjusts their combined weight accordingly — instead of relying on assumptions made months ago.

Safety-First Design

Everything that can go wrong has a planned response

Trading systems fail in specific, predictable ways. oa2 was designed with each failure mode explicitly in mind and a response built in before it was ever deployed:

What could go wrongWhat oa2 does instead
No data available for a debaterThe debater abstains (returns 0 confidence) rather than fabricating a signal
Two experts are perfectly correlated and act as oneTheir combined vote is automatically down-weighted so they can't double-count
Earnings announcement in 2 daysMega-cap trades are hard-blocked 2 trading days before earnings — too unpredictable
Market enters crisis mode suddenlyA leading crisis signal (VIX term structure, vol-of-vol) fires before VIX hits 35 — the system starts reducing size early
Sizing engine rejects every trade (too conservative)Kelly fraction, caps, and CVaR thresholds are calibrated against backtest data to balance safety with activity
Position held too long, market conditions changeRegime-flip exit rule fires within the same trading session the conditions change
moomoo broker unavailableData falls back to yfinance automatically; trade execution pauses until broker reconnects
Bandit has no history (cold start)Pre-loaded with 6 months of historical replay before first live use
Correlation matrix is singular (can't be inverted)Ridge regularisation (tiny constant added to diagonal) ensures the matrix always inverts safely
Hard rule — no override: The sizing engine's three gates (Kelly, Greeks caps, CVaR) cannot be bypassed by any other part of the system. Even if all 6 experts vote unanimously BULLISH with 100% confidence, the trade is still rejected if it would push portfolio risk beyond the safety limits.

A Trade's Full Life Story

From first data point to final close

Here's the complete journey of a single trade — from raw market data to closed position — as a story:

🌅 8:50 AM — Market data arrives

oa2 downloads overnight price data, options chain snapshots, news headlines, and social sentiment for all 22 tickers. Data is validated — any source that looks stale or incomplete is flagged.

🌤️ 9:00 AM — Regime is classified

VIX level, implied vol rank, trend momentum, and cross-asset signals (TLT, GLD, DXY) are fed into the regime classifier. Today's environment is tagged as, say, "VOL_EXPANSION + TRENDING" — bucket #6 of 8. The session clock says we're in the MORNING window (9:30–12:00).

🔍 9:15 AM — Context agents check in

The Dealer agent computes SPY's Gamma Exposure — big banks are net long gamma, meaning they'll dampen any big moves. The Event Risk agent confirms no earnings in the next 48 hours for any ticker we're watching. The Execution Quality agent confirms spreads are normal.

🗣️ 9:30 AM — The 6 experts debate SPY

Each debater independently examines their data slice: Directional sees price above all moving averages (BULLISH ×0.75). Income sees IV Rank at 72% — excellent premium-selling conditions (BULLISH ×0.65). Volatility sees VIX term structure inverted — near-term fear elevated (NEUTRAL). Flow sees a large call sweep on SPY at open (BULLISH ×0.60). Sentiment sees Reddit slightly bearish (BEARISH ×0.40). Dealer sees negative GEX below gamma flip (BULLISH ×0.70).

⚖️ 9:32 AM — Votes are counted (properly)

The consensus engine notes that Directional and Dealer tend to agree with each other often — they share correlation. Their combined vote is down-weighted. After all adjustments, the final result: BULLISH, consensus score 0.66, p_bull = 0.72 (72% probability the bullish trade is right). N_eff = 3.8 effective independent opinions out of 6.

🎯 9:33 AM — Sizing checks begin

Kelly gate: With 72% edge and a 2:1 payout spread (risk $100 to make $200), ¼-Kelly says trade 4 contracts. Book gate: Current portfolio has moderate delta exposure; adding 4 contracts keeps net delta within the 30% cap. All Greek limits pass. CVaR gate: The worst stress scenario (SPY -5% intraday) would cost $280 on 4 contracts — well within the 5% account limit. All three gates: GREEN.

📋 9:34 AM — Exit rules assigned at birth

Before the order is even placed, the exit engine assigns: stop loss at $400 (full max loss), profit target at $200 (50% of max profit), DTE emergency at 2 days, time stop at 21 days, unique trade ID "a3f7-...". These are locked in and cannot be changed later.

📨 9:35 AM — Order placed via moomoo

4 contracts of SPY bull call spread (buy 580 call / sell 590 call, 21 DTE) at the mid-price. Order confirmed. The trade is now in the position monitor.

👁️ Every 5 minutes — Position monitored

The exit engine checks the P&L, current DTE, and current regime on every open position every 5 minutes during market hours. Most checks return "all fine, hold." The position is tracked alongside every other open trade.

✅ Day 11 — Profit target hit

SPY has rallied. The 580/590 call spread is now worth $197 — crossing the $200 profit target (50% of max). Exit rule fires: profit target reached. Close order sent to moomoo. Trade closed. P&L recorded. The bandit is updated: +1 win for Directional in VOL_EXPANSION + TRENDING regime, +1 win for Flow, +1 win for Dealer, etc.

📚 Day 11 — The system learns

Each debater's Beta posterior is updated based on whether they were right in this regime. The consensus engine's EWMA correlation matrix is updated with today's opinion set. The system is incrementally smarter for next time.
